# Portfolio Building with AI — Live Session Preparation

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this live session, you will be able to:

- Present a professional portfolio that showcases your AI capabilities
- Build compelling case studies from project work using AI assistance
- Demonstrate your portfolio to potential clients or employers with confidence
- Identify gaps in your portfolio and create a plan to fill them
- Receive and incorporate peer feedback on your portfolio presentation

---

## Why Your Portfolio Is Everything

In freelancing and AI services, your portfolio is your proof. Clients do not buy promises — they buy evidence that you can deliver. A strong portfolio answers three questions:

1. **Can you do the work?** Show examples of what you have built.
2. **Does it produce results?** Show the impact your work had.
3. **Is it relevant to me?** Show work that resembles what the client needs.

Before this live session, you should have already started building portfolio pieces during Days 21-25. This session focuses on polishing and presenting that work.

## Pre-Session Preparation

Complete these tasks before the live session so you can get the most out of the peer feedback.

### Task 1: Inventory Your Portfolio Pieces

List every project, case study, or work sample you have from the program so far. For each one, note:

- What the project was (one sentence description)
- What AI tools you used
- What the outcome was (results, metrics, or deliverable)
- What you learned from it
- Whether it is complete enough to show

### Task 2: Select Your Strongest Pieces

Choose 2-3 pieces to focus on during the live session. Prioritize:

- Pieces that are complete or near-complete
- Pieces that demonstrate different skills (e.g., one automation project, one content project, one consulting-style project)
- Pieces that have measurable outcomes
- Pieces most relevant to the clients you want to attract

### Task 3: Prepare a Case Study Draft

For each selected piece, write a one-page case study using this structure:

**The STAR Framework for Case Studies:**

- **Situation**: What was the client's (or your own) problem? What was the context?
- **Task**: What specifically were you asked to do? What were the constraints?
- **Action**: What did you do? What AI tools did you use? What was your process?
- **Result**: What was the outcome? Include numbers wherever possible.

**Case Study Template:**

```
## [Project Name]

### The Problem
[1-2 paragraphs describing the situation and pain point]

### The Solution
[2-3 paragraphs describing what you built and how, including AI tools used]

### The Results
- Metric 1: [specific number]
- Metric 2: [specific number]
- Metric 3: [specific number or qualitative outcome]

### Key Learnings
[1-2 sentences about what you would do differently]

### Tools Used
[bulleted list of AI tools and why each was chosen]
```

Use AI to help draft and polish your case studies. Feed it your raw project notes and ask it to structure them into the STAR format. Then refine with your own voice and specific details.

### Task 4: Build a Portfolio Presentation

Prepare a 5-minute presentation of your work. Structure:

1. **Introduction** (30 seconds): Who you are and what you do
2. **Case Study 1** (2 minutes): Problem, solution, results
3. **Case Study 2** (2 minutes): Problem, solution, results
4. **Closing** (30 seconds): What you are looking for next and how to contact you

Practice this presentation before the live session. Use AI to help you refine your talking points, but practice speaking them naturally — do not read from a script.

## Portfolio Platforms

If you do not yet have a place to host your portfolio, choose one before the live session:

### Option 1: Personal Website

Best for: Long-term brand building

- **Carrd**: Simple, affordable, fast to set up ($19/year)
- **Framer**: Beautiful templates, more customization ($5-15/month)
- **Webflow**: Professional-grade, steep learning curve (free tier available)

### Option 2: Portfolio Platforms

Best for: Quick setup and built-in audience

- **Notion**: Free, flexible, easy to update — good for a living portfolio
- **GitHub**: Essential if your work involves code — shows your actual contributions
- **LinkedIn**: Articles and featured section work as a mini-portfolio

### Option 3: PDF Portfolio

Best for: Sending directly to clients

- Create a polished 3-5 page PDF using Canva or AI design tools
- Include 2-3 case studies with visuals
- Update monthly

The best approach is a combination: a living website (Notion or Carrd) as your primary hub, plus a PDF version you can send directly in proposals.

## Portfolio Best Practices

### Show the Process, Not Just the Result

Clients want to see how you think, not just what you deliver. Include:

- Screenshots of your workflow
- Before/after comparisons
- Brief explanations of why you made specific decisions
- Examples of how you used AI tools in your process

### Quantify Everything You Can

Numbers make your work credible:

- "Saved 15 hours per week" is better than "saved a lot of time"
- "Reduced data entry errors by 94%" is better than "improved accuracy"
- "Generated 50 qualified leads in the first month" is better than "helped with lead generation"

If you do not have exact numbers, estimate conservatively and label it as an estimate.

### Keep It Current

Update your portfolio monthly. Remove outdated pieces. Add new projects. Refresh descriptions. A portfolio that has not been updated in six months signals inactivity.

### Make It Easy to Contact You

Every portfolio page should have a clear call to action and your contact information. Do not make people hunt for how to reach you.

## Live Session Activities

During the live session, we will do the following:

### Activity 1: Portfolio Show and Tell (20 minutes)

Each participant presents their portfolio in 5 minutes. The group provides feedback on:

- First impression: Does it look professional?
- Clarity: Can you immediately understand what this person does?
- Proof: Does the work demonstrate capability?
- Relevance: Would you hire this person for your stated niche?

### Activity 2: Case Study Workshop (15 minutes)

We will take one participant's raw project notes and, as a group, structure them into a compelling case study using AI assistance and the STAR framework.

### Activity 3: Gap Analysis (10 minutes)

Each participant identifies:

- What is missing from your portfolio?
- What skills are not yet represented?
- What project could you build this week to fill the biggest gap?

### Activity 4: Peer Feedback Rounds (15 minutes)

Structured peer feedback using the format:

- One thing that is strong (keep doing this)
- One thing that is missing (add this)
- One thing that could be improved (refine this)

---

## Key Takeaways

- Your portfolio is the single most important asset for winning clients — invest in it
- Use the STAR framework (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to structure compelling case studies
- Show process alongside results — clients hire how you think, not just what you make
- Quantify outcomes with specific numbers wherever possible
- Choose a portfolio platform and set it up before seeking clients
- Update your portfolio monthly and make sure every page has a clear call to action
- Use the live session to get honest feedback and identify gaps

## Practice Challenge

**Complete Before the Live Session (60 minutes)**

1. **Portfolio Inventory**: List all your project pieces with one-sentence descriptions, AI tools used, and outcomes achieved.

2. **Case Study Writing**: Write two complete case studies using the STAR framework and the template provided. Each should be 300-500 words with at least two quantified results.

3. **Portfolio Setup**: If you do not have a portfolio platform, set one up today. Add your case studies, a brief bio, and your contact information. It does not need to be perfect — it needs to exist.

4. **Presentation Prep**: Write and practice a 5-minute presentation of your work. Time yourself. Record it if possible and review for clarity and pacing.

5. **Self-Critique**: Look at your portfolio through the eyes of a potential client. Answer these questions honestly:
   - Can I tell what this person does within 10 seconds?
   - Do I see proof that they can deliver results?
   - Would I trust them with my project?
   - Is there a clear way to contact them?

Submit your two case studies and portfolio link (or screenshots if not yet online) for review before the live session.
