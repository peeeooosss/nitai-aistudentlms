## Session Preparation: The Communication Gap in AI

The biggest barrier to AI adoption isn't technology — it's communication. AI practitioners and business stakeholders often talk past each other:

- **AI teams** say: "We achieved 0.87 AUC-ROC on our validation set with a gradient boosted ensemble."
- **Executives** hear: "Something technical that might work."
- **Executives** want to know: "Will this save us money? How much? When?"

Bridging this gap is an essential skill for AI leaders.

### Stakeholder Communication Framework

**The "So What" Principle:**

Every technical result should be translated through three layers:

```
Layer 1: Technical Result
"We improved model accuracy from 78% to 87%."

Layer 2: Operational Impact  
"This means the system correctly identifies 9 more out of 10 at-risk customers."

Layer 3: Business Value
"Those 9 additional customers represent $2.3M in annual revenue that we can now retain through proactive outreach."
```

### Stakeholder Mapping for AI Projects

| Stakeholder | What They Care About | How to Communicate | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CEO/Board** | ROI, competitive advantage, risk | Executive summary, business impact metrics | Monthly/Quarterly |
| **CFO** | Cost, ROI, budget adherence | Financial metrics, cost-benefit analysis | Monthly |
| **CTO** | Architecture, scalability, technical debt | Technical deep-dives, architecture reviews | Bi-weekly |
| **Business Unit Leaders** | Solutions to their problems | Use case demos, workflow improvements | Weekly |
| **End Users** | Ease of use, time savings, reliability | Training, feedback sessions, demos | As needed |
| **Legal/Compliance** | Regulatory risk, bias, privacy | Compliance reports, audit results | Monthly |
| **IT Operations** | Stability, security, supportability | Deployment plans, runbooks, SLAs | Per deployment |
| **HR** | Impact on workforce, skills needs | Workforce plans, training programs | Quarterly |

### Storytelling with AI Data

**The Problem-Solution-Impact Narrative:**

```
1. PROBLEM (Make it relatable)
"Every month, our customer success team manually reviews 500 accounts 
to identify which might churn. They spend 40 hours on this, and they 
miss 30% of at-risk customers."

2. SOLUTION (Make it understandable)
"We built a system that analyzes customer behavior patterns — login 
frequency, support tickets, usage trends — and automatically scores 
every account for churn risk. It's like having a senior analyst 
reviewing every account every day."

3. IMPACT (Make it quantifiable)
"In our test, the AI system identified 85% of customers who actually 
churned, compared to 70% by the human team. Applied to our full 
customer base, this represents $4.2M in annual revenue that we can 
now protect. The system costs $180K per year to operate."
```

## Live Exercises

### Exercise 1: Executive Presentation

**Scenario:** You have 15 minutes to present AI project results to the executive team. The results are mixed:
- Model accuracy: 82% (target was 85%)
- Time savings: 60% reduction in manual processing (target was 50%)
- Cost: $340K vs. $300K budgeted
- Timeline: 2 weeks behind schedule
- Bias audit: Passed with one area needing attention

**Your task (individual, then group review):**
Prepare and deliver a 5-minute presentation of these results. Your presentation must:
1. Lead with the positive (what's working)
2. Be honest about shortfalls
3. Explain the root causes
4. Present a plan to address gaps
5. End with a clear ask (what you need from the executives)

### Exercise 2: Difficult Conversation Roleplay

**Scenario Pairs:** Practice these conversations:

**Conversation 1 — The Skeptical CFO:**
The CFO says: "You spent $300K on this AI project and it's only 82% accurate? My spreadsheet is 100% accurate and it cost nothing. Why should I approve more funding?"

**Conversation 2 — The Excited CEO:**
The CEO says: "I just read about ChatGPT in the Wall Street Journal. Can't we just use that to automate everything? Why are we building custom models?"

**Conversation 3 — The Worried Manager:**
A department manager says: "I heard AI is going to replace my team. I'm not going to cooperate with your project if it means my people lose their jobs."

**Conversation 4 — The Impatient Sponsor:**
Your executive sponsor says: "The board is asking for AI wins. Your project has been running for 3 months and I haven't seen anything. Should we cut our losses?"

### Exercise 3: Communication Plan Design

**Scenario:** You're launching an enterprise AI program across 5 departments. Design a comprehensive communication plan:

**Deliverables:**
1. Stakeholder register with communication preferences
2. Communication cadence calendar (first 90 days)
3. Key messages for each stakeholder group
4. Feedback collection mechanisms
5. Escalation procedures
6. Success story template for internal marketing

## Discussion Topics

### Topic 1: Managing AI Hype

How do you balance enthusiasm about AI's potential with realistic expectations? When executives read headlines about AI revolutionizing everything, how do you calibrate expectations without killing excitement?

### Topic 2: Communicating Failures

When an AI experiment fails to show improvement, how do you communicate this? What's the line between being honest about setbacks and maintaining confidence in the program?

### Topic 3: The AI Champion Network

How do you build a network of AI champions across the organization? Who are the best candidates, and how do you equip them to advocate for AI in their departments?

---

## Key Takeaways

1. The "So What" principle translates technical results into business value
2. Different stakeholders need different messages, formats, and cadences
3. Storytelling with data (Problem-Solution-Impact) is the most effective presentation format
4. Honesty about shortcomings builds more trust than hiding problems
5. A stakeholder communication plan should be created before the project starts, not after
6. Building a champion network amplifies your communication reach
