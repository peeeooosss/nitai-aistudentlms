## Review: The Five Pillars (Days 21-25)

This quiz covers everything from the first five days of our client-facing curriculum. Let us recap the core concepts from each day.

### Day 21: Building a Personal Brand with AI

**Core concepts to remember:**

- Personal Brand = Expertise + Consistency + Visibility + Trust
- Brand foundations come first: niche, value proposition, and voice
- The Trust Stack: Knowledge → Results → Process → Social Proof → Consistency
- Content follows the 80/20 rule: 80% value, 20% promotion
- AI accelerates branding but cannot replace strategy

**Key frameworks:**

- Brand Equation: Expertise + Consistency + Visibility + Trust
- Brand Voice Document: Tone, Vocabulary, Style, Personality
- Trust Stack: Five layers of credibility building

### Day 22: AI-Driven Lead Generation

**Core concepts to remember:**

- Lead generation funnel: Awareness → Interest → Evaluation → Conversion
- Lead magnets must deliver genuine value, not just collect emails
- Personalization Pyramid: Name → Role → Company → Trigger → Insight
- The insight-level personalization is the gold standard
- Consistent weekly workflow beats sporadic effort

**Key frameworks:**

- Lead Generation Funnel (4 stages)
- Personalization Pyramid (5 levels)
- Weekly Lead Generation Workflow (Mon-Fri plan)
- Target metrics: 80 leads, 40 outreach, 10 conversations, 3 proposals, 1 close per month

### Day 23: Client Communication with AI

**Core concepts to remember:**

- Five phases of client communication: First Contact, Discovery, Proposal, Execution, Delivery
- Discovery is where projects are won or lost
- Templates save time without losing quality (80% template, 20% personal)
- Difficult conversations require preparation — use AI as a rehearsal partner
- The proposal is both a sales document and a project plan

**Key frameworks:**

- Client Communication Lifecycle (5 phases)
- Discovery Framework: Problem, Impact, Vision, Constraints, Stakeholders
- Proposal Structure: Executive Summary, Scope, Investment, Process, About, Next Steps

### Day 24: Freelancing with AI Tools

**Core concepts to remember:**

- AI is an efficiency multiplier — same quality, fraction of the time
- Build a focused tool stack: AI assistants, project management, communication, content, finance
- Complete workflow: Lead → Discovery → Proposal → Execution → Delivery → Follow-up
- Batch similar tasks, automate what you can, set boundaries
- Price based on value, not hours

**Key frameworks:**

- AI Freelance Tool Stack (5 categories)
- Complete Project Workflow (6 steps)
- Efficiency Multiplier Table (time savings by project phase)

### Day 25: Pricing Your AI Services

**Core concepts to remember:**

- Four pricing models: Hourly, Project, Value-Based, Retainer
- Value-based pricing is the ideal for AI services
- Three-tier packages: Starter, Standard, Premium
- Pricing psychology: Anchoring, Rule of 100, Decoy Pricing, Framing
- Never discount without adjusting scope

**Key frameworks:**

- Hourly Rate Formula: (Income + Expenses + Taxes) / Billable Hours
- Three-Tier Package Framework
- Proposal Close Rate Target: 30-40%

## Practice Question Bank

These questions mirror the format you will see on the quiz. Work through each one.

### Multiple Choice

**Q1**: Which element of the Personal Brand Equation is hardest for AI to create?
- A) Expertise
- B) Visibility
- C) Trust
- D) Consistency

<details><summary>Answer</summary>C) Trust — AI can accelerate content production and scheduling, but trust is built through genuine results and relationships over time.</details>

**Q2**: At what level of the Personalization Pyramid does outreach become truly effective?
- A) Name-based personalization
- B) Role-based personalization
- C) Trigger-based personalization
- D) Insight-based personalization

<details><summary>Answer</summary>D) Insight-based personalization — when you demonstrate specific knowledge about the prospect's situation, you stand out from everyone else sending generic messages.</details>

**Q3**: What percentage of proposals should you aim to close?
- A) 10-20%
- B) 30-40%
- C) 60-70%
- D) 90-100%

<details><summary>Answer</summary>B) 30-40% — this indicates your pricing is in the right range: high enough to earn well, low enough to stay competitive.</details>

### Short Answer

**Q4**: A client asks for a 20% discount on your $5,000 project proposal. Write your response (2-3 sentences).

<details><summary>Sample Answer</summary>"I appreciate you sharing your budget concerns. I can adjust the scope to fit a $4,000 budget — we could start with two of the three workflows and add the third in a Phase 2. Alternatively, I can offer a payment plan that spreads the $5,000 over three months."</details>

**Q5**: Describe the Discovery Framework and explain why each element matters.

<details><summary>Sample Answer</summary>The Discovery Framework has five elements: Problem (what is broken), Impact (what it costs them), Vision (what success looks like), Constraints (budget, timeline, tech), and Stakeholders (who decides). Each element prevents scope creep, ensures alignment, and builds the foundation for a proposal that addresses their actual needs rather than your assumptions.</details>

### Scenario-Based

**Q6**: You are building a lead magnet for small business owners who struggle with manual data entry. What type of lead magnet would you create and why?

<details><summary>Sample Answer</summary>A checklist or template would work well here — it is low effort to create and delivers a quick, actionable win. For example, "The 10-Point Data Automation Audit" that helps business owners identify which processes they should automate first. This positions you as the expert and naturally leads to a conversation about your services.</details>

## Building Your First AI Offer

Now let us put it all together. By the end of this exercise, you will have a complete, launchable offer.

### The Offer Canvas

Complete each section:

**1. Service Description** (What do you do?)
Write one sentence: "I help [target audience] achieve [specific outcome] using [your AI methodology]."

**2. Target Client** (Who do you serve?)
Describe your ideal client in 2-3 sentences: industry, size, role, and primary pain point.

**3. Packages** (What are your options?)
List your three tiers with names, key deliverables, and prices.

**4. Proof** (Why should they trust you?)
List three forms of proof you can present: personal projects, case studies, process documentation, testimonials, or certifications.

**5. Distribution** (How will clients find you?)
List three channels you will use for lead generation and how you will use AI to support each.

**6. Communication** (How will you close deals?)
Describe your proposal process and the templates you will use.

## Practice Challenge

**Prepare for the Quiz and Build Your Offer (60 minutes)**

1. **Self-Assessment Review**: Go through all 20 self-assessment questions above. For any you cannot answer confidently, review that day's material before moving on.

2. **Practice Questions**: Complete the practice question bank. For each question, write your answer before looking at the provided answer.

3. **Offer Canvas**: Complete the full Offer Canvas exercise above. Be specific — no vague descriptions. Your offer should be something you could present to a real client tomorrow.

4. **Elevator Pitch**: Write a 30-second elevator pitch for your AI service. Use AI to help refine it, but make sure it sounds like you. Practice saying it out loud five times.

5. **Weak Spot Identification**: Identify your two weakest areas from the past five days. Write a specific plan for how you will strengthen them before the quiz.

Submit your completed Offer Canvas and elevator pitch for review. These become the foundation for the live session on Day 27.
