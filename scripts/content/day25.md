# Pricing Your AI Services

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Understand different pricing models and when to use each one
- Calculate your minimum viable rate and set profitable prices
- Create tiered service packages that appeal to different client budgets
- Price based on value delivered rather than hours worked
- Handle pricing conversations and negotiations with confidence

---

## The Pricing Problem

Pricing is where most freelancers leave the most money on the table. Three common mistakes:

1. **Charging hourly**: Penalizes you for getting faster (exactly what AI makes you)
2. **Copying competitors**: Their prices reflect their costs and goals, not yours
3. **Undervaluing because you are new**: Your price should reflect the value you deliver, not your years of experience

AI changes the pricing equation fundamentally. If you used to spend 10 hours on a task and now spend 3 hours using AI, but the client gets the same (or better) result, you should earn more per hour, not less.

---

## Pricing Models for AI Services

### Model 1: Hourly Rate

**When to use it**: Short-term tasks, uncertain scope, or when the client insists.

**How to calculate**:

```
Hourly Rate = (Annual Income Goal + Annual Expenses + Taxes) / Billable Hours per Year
```

Example:
- Income goal: $80,000
- Expenses (tools, insurance, marketing): $12,000
- Taxes (estimate 25%): $23,000
- Total needed: $115,000
- Billable hours: 1,200 (30 hours/week x 40 weeks)
- Minimum hourly rate: $96/hour

**The problem**: As AI makes you faster, your effective hourly rate drops unless you raise your rate. This model does not scale.

### Model 2: Project-Based Pricing

**When to use it**: Well-defined deliverables with clear scope.

**How to set the price**:

1. Estimate how long the project takes (including AI assistance)
2. Calculate your hourly equivalent for that time
3. Add 20-30% for project management, revisions, and risk
4. Research what the market pays for similar deliverables
5. Set the price based on the value to the client, not just your time

**Advantage**: As you get faster with AI, your profit margin increases on every project.

### Model 3: Value-Based Pricing

**When to use it**: High-impact projects where you can quantify the client's gain.

**How to set the price**:

1. Understand the financial impact of the problem you are solving
2. Set your price as a percentage of that impact (typically 10-30%)
3. Present the ROI to the client: "This investment of $X will save you $Y per year"

Example: You build an AI automation system that saves a company 20 hours per week of manual data entry. If the employee doing that work costs $25/hour, that is $500/week or $26,000/year in savings. Charging $8,000 for the system is an obvious win for both sides.

### Model 4: Retainer Pricing

**When to use it**: Ongoing services where the client needs regular AI support.

**How to set the price**:

1. Define the scope of ongoing work (hours, deliverables, or access)
2. Price at a slight discount compared to project rates (10-15%)
3. Set clear terms: what is included, what is extra, and cancellation terms

**Advantage**: Predictable monthly income. This is the most stable model for freelancers.

## Creating Service Packages

Packages make pricing easier for both you and your client. Instead of custom quotes for every project, you offer predefined tiers.

### The Three-Tier Package Framework

| | **Starter** | **Standard** | **Premium** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ideal for** | Small businesses testing AI | Growing companies ready to invest | Enterprises needing full solutions |
| **Includes** | 1 AI automation workflow | 3 workflows + documentation | Full AI strategy + implementation |
| **Timeline** | 1-2 weeks | 3-4 weeks | 6-8 weeks |
| **Support** | Email only | Email + weekly calls | Dedicated Slack channel |
| **Revisions** | 1 round | 2 rounds | Unlimited |
| **Price Range** | $1,500 - $3,000 | $4,000 - $8,000 | $10,000 - $25,000+ |

**Why three tiers work:**

- Most people choose the middle option (anchoring effect)
- The low tier gets clients in the door
- The high tier makes the middle tier feel affordable
- You capture value from clients with different budgets

### How to Build Your Packages

1. **List every service you could offer** — brainstorm without limits
2. **Group them into logical clusters** — what naturally goes together?
3. **Define three tiers** — basic, comprehensive, and premium
4. **Price each tier** using the value-based model as your guide
5. **Write a clear description** of what each tier includes (and what it does not)

Use AI to help draft package descriptions, refine your pricing logic, and analyze competitor packages.

## Pricing Psychology

A few principles that influence how clients perceive your prices:

### Anchoring

The first number a client sees sets their expectation. Always present your premium package first or include a "most popular" label on your mid-tier.

### The Rule of 100

For prices under $100, show the dollar amount ($47). For prices over $100, show the annual equivalent ($4.70/day). This makes larger numbers feel smaller.

### Decoy Pricing

Adding a third option that is clearly inferior makes the target option look better. If you only have two options, add a third that is priced close to one but with fewer features.

### Price Framing

How you describe the price matters as much as the number:

- "$5,000" sounds like a lot
- "A one-time investment of $5,000 that saves you $26,000/year" sounds like a bargain
- "Less than $14/day" reframes the same amount into something manageable

## Handling Pricing Conversations

### When a Client Asks "How Much?"

Never give a price without understanding the scope first. Response:

- "I want to give you an accurate number. Can you tell me more about what you need? A few quick questions will help me put together the right recommendation."

### When a Client Says "That Is Too Expensive"

Do not immediately discount. Instead:

1. Ask what budget they had in mind
2. Ask what outcome they are expecting
3. Offer to adjust the scope to fit their budget
4. Show the ROI: "If this saves you $X per year, the investment pays for itself in Y months"
5. If they truly cannot afford it, refer them to a lower-cost alternative (this builds goodwill)

### When a Client Wants a Discount

- Never discount without removing something from the scope
- Offer a payment plan instead of a discount
- Offer a referral incentive instead of a discount
- Stand firm: "My pricing reflects the value and quality of the work. I want to make sure I can deliver at the level you deserve."

## Setting Your Rates: A Practical Exercise

Use this framework to set your initial rates:

1. **Calculate your floor**: The minimum you need to earn per hour to cover costs and goals
2. **Research the ceiling**: What the market pays for comparable services
3. **Find your position**: Where between floor and ceiling do you want to be?
4. **Test and adjust**: Start at your target rate. If you are closing more than 50% of proposals, your prices may be too low. If you are closing less than 20%, they may be too high.

The sweet spot is where you are closing about 30-40% of proposals — high enough to earn well, low enough to stay competitive.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Hourly pricing penalizes you for getting faster with AI — shift to project-based, value-based, or retainer pricing
- Calculate your minimum rate, then price based on the value you deliver to the client
- Three-tier packages simplify pricing decisions and capture value from different budgets
- Use pricing psychology (anchoring, framing, decoys) to present your prices effectively
- Never discount without adjusting scope — offer payment plans or referral incentives instead
- Test your rates by tracking proposal close rates: aim for 30-40% acceptance
- The goal is not to be the cheapest — it is to be the best value

1. **Rate Calculation**: Using the formula provided, calculate your minimum hourly rate. Be honest about your income goals and expenses.

2. **Package Creation**: Build three service packages (Starter, Standard, Premium) for your AI services. Write a clear description of what each includes, the timeline, and the price.

   - A client asks for your cheapest option
