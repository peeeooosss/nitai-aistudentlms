## Session Preparation: Recap & Foundation

### What Is an AI Transformation Roadmap?

An AI transformation roadmap is a strategic document that outlines how an organization will systematically adopt AI across its operations over a defined timeline. It connects AI capabilities to business goals, sequences initiatives by priority, and defines success metrics.

The roadmap is not a project plan — it is a strategic narrative that answers: Where are we now, where do we want to be, and how will we get there?

### The Three Horizons Framework

Borrowed from McKinsey's Three Horizons of Growth, this framework applies well to AI transformation:

**Horizon 1 (0-12 months): Quick Wins**
- Use cases with high business value and low technical complexity
- Build organizational confidence and AI literacy
- Establish foundational data and governance infrastructure
- Examples: document automation, chatbots for internal IT support, email classification

**Horizon 2 (12-24 months): Strategic Initiatives**
- More complex use cases requiring deeper data integration
- Cross-functional AI solutions
- Formal MLOps and governance in place
- Examples: customer churn prediction, supply chain optimization, predictive maintenance

**Horizon 3 (24-36+ months): Transformational**
- AI fundamentally changes business models or creates new revenue streams
- Requires mature data infrastructure and talent
- Examples: autonomous decision systems, AI-native products, real-time personalization engines

### Value vs. Feasibility Matrix

Before sequencing initiatives, map each potential use case on two axes:

```
                    HIGH FEASIBILITY
                          │
    ┌─────────────────────┼─────────────────────┐
    │                     │                     │
    │   QUICK WINS        │   MAJOR PROJECTS    │
    │   (Do first)        │   (Plan carefully)  │
    │                     │                     │
HIGH│─────────────────────┼─────────────────────│HIGH
VALUE│                    │                     │VALUE
    │                     │                     │
    │   FILL-INS          │   RECONSIDER        │
    │   (Delegate/automate)│  (Deprioritize)    │
    │                     │                     │
    └─────────────────────┼─────────────────────┘
                          │
                    LOW FEASIBILITY
```

## Live Exercises

### Exercise 1: Enterprise Readiness Assessment

You are consulting for a mid-size manufacturing company (2,000 employees, $500M revenue) that wants to adopt AI. The CEO has asked you to create a roadmap. Here is what you know:

- Legacy ERP system (SAP ECC, not yet on S/4HANA)
- Data stored in silos: production data in historian, customer data in CRM, financial data in ERP
- No dedicated data science team
- Strong IT department but focused on maintenance
- Board wants to see AI initiatives within 6 months
- Annual technology budget: $8M, with $1.5M earmarked for innovation

**Your task (groups of 3-4):**
1. Conduct a readiness assessment across the five pillars
2. Identify 5-8 potential AI use cases
3. Place each use case on the value-feasibility matrix
4. Propose a three-horizon roadmap with specific milestones

### Exercise 2: Roadmap Presentation

Each group will prepare a 5-minute executive presentation of their roadmap. Your presentation must include:

- Current state summary (1 slide)
- Proposed use cases with expected ROI (1 slide)
- Three-horizon timeline (1 slide)
- Resource requirements and budget (1 slide)
- Risk mitigation approach (1 slide)

### Exercise 3: Objection Handling

The following objections will be raised. Prepare responses:

- "Our competitors are already using AI. We're behind." — How do you respond without overcommitting?
- "We don't have clean data. Shouldn't we fix that first?" — How long should data remediation take before starting AI projects?
- "The board wants results in 6 months." — How do you set realistic expectations while maintaining momentum?
- "What if the AI makes a bad decision?" — How do you address governance concerns?
- "We tried AI two years ago and it didn't work." — How do you rebuild confidence?

## Discussion Topics

### Topic 1: Build vs. Buy vs. Partner

For each horizon, the build/buy/partner calculus changes:
- **Horizon 1**: Lean toward buy (SaaS AI tools) and partner (consulting firms)
- **Horizon 2**: Mix of buy and build; develop internal capabilities
- **Horizon 3**: Primarily build for competitive differentiation

What factors influence this decision? Discuss the tradeoffs between speed-to-value and long-term capability building.

### Topic 2: Cultural Change Management

Technical implementation is only 40% of AI transformation. The rest is people and process change.

Questions to explore:
- How do you get frontline employees excited about AI rather than threatened by it?
- What role does middle management play in AI adoption?
- How do you handle resistance from department heads who feel their expertise is being devalued?

### Topic 3: Governance Without Stifling Innovation

Too much governance slows everything down. Too little creates risk.

- What governance checkpoints are non-negotiable?
- How do you create a "safe sandbox" for experimentation within a governed environment?
- Who should have veto power over AI deployments?

## Key Takeaways

1. AI transformation requires a phased approach — trying to do everything at once leads to failure
2. The value-feasibility matrix helps prioritize initiatives objectively
3. Quick wins in Horizon 1 build organizational confidence for larger investments later
4. Cultural change management is as important as technical implementation
5. Governance should be proportionate — enable innovation while managing risk
6. Executive sponsorship is the single biggest predictor of AI transformation success
